04 • STABILITY

Duplet and Octet Rules

2

Duplet Rule

Atoms with only the first shell are especially stable when it contains two electrons, like helium.

8

Octet Rule

Many atoms tend to gain, lose or share electrons to achieve eight electrons in their outermost shell.

08 • EXAMPLE 1

Formation of Sodium Chloride

Na + Cl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻

Step 1

Sodium has one valence electron.

Step 2

Chlorine needs one electron to complete its octet.

Step 3

Sodium transfers one electron to chlorine.

The oppositely charged ions attract and form sodium chloride (NaCl).

09 • EXAMPLE 2

Formation of Magnesium Oxide

Mg + O → Mg²⁺ + O²⁻
  • Magnesium has two valence electrons.
  • Oxygen needs two electrons to complete its octet.
  • Magnesium transfers two electrons to oxygen.
  • The resulting ions attract to form magnesium oxide (MgO).

16 • COMPARISON

Ionic vs Covalent Bond

Ionic Bond

• Electron transfer

• Usually metal + non-metal

• Forms ions

• Often high melting points

• Conducts electricity when molten or dissolved if mobile ions are present

Covalent Bond

• Electron sharing

• Usually non-metal + non-metal

• Forms molecules or networks

• Molecular substances often have lower melting points

• Usually poor electrical conductors
